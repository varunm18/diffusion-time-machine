What the multi-scene model learns from

Every photo here is a real, dated photo of a famous place, taken from Wikimedia Commons, with a camera pose from MegaScenes. The model sees several photos of one place, plus a date, and learns to draw the place as it looked on that date. This page sums up the data in the training run that started on 25 Nov.

What a training step actually sees

One training sample is 8 photos of one place: 3 reference photos the model can look at, and 5 target photos it has to draw, all with the targets' date. The targets are built around one anchor photo plus the photos that see the same part of the place from the same period.

Black-and-white is not a date

The 11 held-out places

The model never trains on these. They test whether a date sweep works on a place it has never seen. The ones with old photos give a real "then" to compare against.

Words used on this page

Usable photo
Has a camera pose, a date, is not a duplicate and is not excluded (for example a rolled or broken image).
Reliable depth
Its estimated depth map agrees with the 3D points of the place. Only these photos can be references; any usable photo can be a target.
Old
Taken before 1970. Each place's eras are cut at 1970; Notre-Dame also has a cut at the April 2019 fire.
Anchor
A photo a training sample can be built around: it needs enough photos from the same period that see the same part of the place.
Held-out photos
Inside training places, about 10% of photos per era are kept aside for checking (Notre-Dame: 132 photos in whole groups).
Dates
From the file's metadata or title. They are mostly reliable (average confidence 0.92 out of 1); some old photos only have a range, like "1890s", and we use the middle of it.

Numbers come from the scene folders (views.parquet, depth_stats.parquet) via scripts/mvgen/dataset_stats.py, and from the sampling table that the run printed at start-up (job 8577831).
