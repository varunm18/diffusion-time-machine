# Pipeline audit (2026-11-19)

**Question:** nothing we trained beat plain SEVA. Why?

**Answer in one paragraph.** Every training run so far optimised numerical noise. Training ran in half precision (fp16, then bf16), where the gradient reaching our adapters is rounding noise hundreds to thousands of times larger than the true gradient and points in an unrelated direction. In full precision (fp32) the same pipeline learns at once. Two more problems made the picture worse: we rendered with 8 frames, which is SEVA's known artefact regime (it always runs 21), and many training samples ask for targets the inputs do not show. The model code, cameras, conditioning and loss are otherwise correct.

How the audit was run: three independent reviewers (SEVA usage, training code, data and cameras), each given the code and the symptom but not our theories, plus GPU tests on plain SEVA and on the training loop. Scripts and raw outputs are in `data/audit_2026-11-19/` (not in git).

---

## 1. Root cause: half-precision training gives a noise gradient

**The measurement** (`data/audit_2026-11-19/training_code/gpu_check_grad_precision.py`, 3 training samples × 5 noise levels, plain SEVA with zero-initialised adapters, the real EDM-weighted loss):

| Noise level (σ) | fp32 gradient norm (FiLM / LoRA) | bf16 norm | bf16 cosine with fp32 | fp16 norm | fp16 cosine with fp32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 (0.9) | 0.59 / 0.29 | 18 / 7 | +0.35 / +0.07 | 203 / 91 | +0.19 / −0.01 |
| 400 (6.4) | 0.86 / 0.33 | 1,080 / 311 | +0.04 / −0.01 | 593 / 183 | +0.29 / +0.19 |
| 700 (19.9) | 1.11 / 0.45 | 3,420 / 913 | −0.01 / −0.02 | 795 / 204 | +0.34 / +0.02 |
| 900 (48.6) | 2.05 / 1.02 | 2,250 / 564 | −0.12 / +0.03 | 465 / 143 | +0.02 / +0.05 |
| 990 (80.4) | 6.46 / 2.20 | 2,700 / 743 | +0.03 / +0.01 | 574 / 117 | +0.10 / +0.04 |

- The true gradient has norm about 1. The bf16 "gradient" has norm about 1,000 and is uncorrelated with it. fp16 is no better.
- A rerun with the inputs changed by one part in a million gives an unrelated bf16 gradient (cosine about 0).
- Our logs showed gradient norms of 700–1,200 in every run ("clipped 100%"). We had put that down to the loss weighting. It was this.

**Why it happens.** SEVA predicts the noise. At medium and high noise its error is only about 1–2% of its output, the same size as bf16's rounding (0.4%, 8 significant bits). The loss is built from that small difference, so its gradient is mostly rounding error, and the backward pass adds more. The adapters' small forward corrections are also partly rounded away when added to half-precision activations.

**Corroborating evidence**

| Test | Half precision | fp32 |
|---|---|---|
| One fixed sample, fixed noise level and noise (fully deterministic), LoRA lr 1e-4, 60 steps: loss at levels 150 / 500 | −11% / −6%, then stalls and jitters | −40% / −34%, still falling |
| One fixed sample, random noise levels (as in training), 200 steps: loss at levels 150–850 | −3% to −11%, stalled | −23% to −29%, still falling |
| Two pinned samples through the real trainer, 600 steps (section 6) | loss on those samples **up** 3–25% | loss down 32–51% |
| Two bf16 runs with the same seed, samples and noise: cosine between their weight changes | +0.02 to +0.08 (fp16 twin: +0.63) | n/a |
| Weight movement vs a pure random walk (1.0 = noise only) | 0.97–1.08 in every bf16 run | n/a |
| Averaging 4 checkpoints of a run | no better than the individual ones (systematic drift, not zero-mean noise) | n/a |

**History.** Runs before 2026-11-10 used fp16 without a gradient scaler; the code audit of that date switched to bf16, which is coarser. Neither could learn. The earlier conclusion "precision is ruled out (fp16 = bf16)" compared two broken settings.

**Fix (done).** Training and the loss probes now default to fp32 (`--autocast fp32`). SEVA's attention is un-pinned from the half-precision-only flash kernel so it can run in fp32. Resuming a checkpoint at a different precision is refused. Cost: about 16 GB instead of 14 GB, and about 5 s per step instead of 2.8 s (LoRA + FiLM, 8 views, A6000).

## 2. Rendering with 8 frames is SEVA's artefact regime

- SEVA's released weights were last trained at 21 frames, and its own pipeline always runs 21: fewer frames are padded by repeating the last one. Its paper says running fewer without padding "leads to abnormal color overflows".
- We rendered every probe with 8 frames. Plain SEVA, mean PSNR against the real photos (5 view sets × 2 noise seeds):

| Setting | Easy same-year samples | Fire probe |
|---|---|---|
| 8 frames, guidance 2 (all our probes) | 9.6 dB | 9.9 dB |
| 8 frames, guidance 1 | 8.3 dB (fully fragmented) | 8.2 dB |
| 21 frames, guidance 2 (official) | **11.6 dB** | 10.8 dB |
| 21 frames, guidance 1 | 11.2 dB (coherent) | 11.6 dB |

- So the "fragmented, over-saturated" look we spent days on is largely this, and "SEVA needs guidance to avoid fragments" was an 8-frame artefact too.
- The single-step denoising loss does not show it: with 21 distinct views it is only 2–7% lower than with 8. The damage builds up in the sampler.
- Re-rendering two "broken" FiLM checkpoints at 21 frames removes the fragmentation. What remains is real damage from training: 2015 renders as a brown-green blur, 2021 is degraded; 1850, 1900 and "unknown" are clean.

**Fix (done).** `generate_targets` and the render probes pad to 21 frames by default (`pad_to`, `--render-frames`, `render_probes.py --frames`).

**Still to decide:** train at 21 frames too (the loss-level effect is small, so this is lower priority).

## 3. Many training samples are ill-posed

Measured on Notre-Dame with COLMAP's 3D points, 1,000 samples drawn exactly as training draws them.

| Sample type | Targets sharing essentially nothing with their 3 inputs (lenient – strict) |
|---|---|
| All training samples | 24% – 74% |
| Modern targets, old inputs (the 30-year gap enforced) | 42% – 97% |
| Old targets | 20% – 77% |
| Easy baseline (8 nearest same-date cameras) | 9% – 25% |

- "Strict" counts shared COLMAP points (it undercounts across eras); "lenient" counts surface overlap (it overcounts). The truth is in between.
- 29% of training targets share no COLMAP point at all with their inputs.
- Plain SEVA confirms it: on training-style samples, wrong camera conventions change the loss by about 1% and scrambling the target cameras by only 4–6% (12–18% on easy samples). The inputs carry little usable geometry there.

**Why**
- Inputs are picked near one anchor target only; the other four targets can be anywhere.
- `max_neighbors=24` does nothing for old targets: an old date group has a median of 12 views, from every side of the building.
- With the gap enforced, modern targets must use old inputs, and only about 107 views predate 1985, mostly of the west facade.
- "Nearest camera" ignores field of view: a telephoto close-up counts as a neighbour of a wide shot (3.4× difference at the median within one sample).

**Probes affected:** `old_from_modern` is ill-posed (inputs are statue close-ups covering about 2% of what the targets show). `modern_from_old` overlaps only partly. `fire` is well-posed.

**Not fixed yet.** See section 8.

## 4. Evaluation artefacts that misled us

- **One noise seed.** A frame's brightness follows the mean of its initial sampler noise (R² = 0.78). Seed 0, used by every training probe, has an extreme draw on the 4th target: it is dark in 9 of 9 plain-SEVA seed-0 sheets across four scenes. We had called it "a hard view".
- **Step-0 baseline.** Plain SEVA already fragments on the harder probes at 8 frames; probes must be read against their step-0 render.
- **Guidance and steps.** We use guidance 2.0 (the bottom of SEVA's recommended 2–5) and 25 sampler steps (SEVA: 50).
- **"Memorisation".** The single-scene runs never reproduced their training photos; the claim came from probe numbers drifting. With a noise gradient, that drift was a random walk.

## 6. Proof that the fixed pipeline learns

The basic sanity test that had never been run: two pinned samples with the same three pre-fire inputs (A: post-fire targets, 2019–24; B: pre-fire targets, 2010–17), trained through the real trainer for 600 steps.

| Run | bf16: loss on the two samples | fp32: loss on the two samples |
|---|---|---|
| LoRA + FiLM, lr 1e-4 | +3% to +25% (worse) | **−32% to −51%** |
| LoRA + FiLM, lr 1e-5 | about ±3% | −14% to −31% |
| FiLM only, lr 1e-4 | +6% to +18% (worse) | −14% to −32% |

- fp32 gradient norms during these runs: 0.3–0.9 (bf16: about 1,000).
- **Renders:** the fp32 lr 1e-4 run reproduces the pre-fire sample's training photos (the orange night-lit facade, the blossom tree, the rose-window close-up) and moves the post-fire sample toward its overcast look. The same run in bf16 renders white mush.
- **First real date effect.** In the FiLM-only and lr 1e-5 runs the correct date beats the wrong one: for the pre-fire sample at every noise level (by 4–8 points), for the post-fire sample at high noise. The lr 1e-4 LoRA run fits both samples without the date, because their target cameras differ.
- This tests the machinery on two samples. It says nothing yet about generalisation.
- Loss weighting, now that it can be tested (one fixed sample, fp32, 300 steps): plain noise-MSE lowers the loss 31–37% at every noise level; the EDM weighting lowers it 30% at low noise and 46–54% at high noise, as it was designed to.

## 7. Checked and correct

- **Cameras:** reprojection error against COLMAP is 0.36 px at the median over all 925 views (95th percentile 1.2 px), through pose conversion, intrinsics, resize, crop and undistortion. The convention is OpenCV, as SEVA expects. On easy samples the correct cameras give the lowest loss of 13 variants.
- **Conditioning:** the tensors our training path feeds the network equal the ones SEVA's own sampler builds (difference about 1e-6), including the unconditional branch.
- **Loss:** input and output scaling, noise levels, the `replace` mechanism and the timestep index match SEVA's denoiser exactly.
- **Weights, VAE, CLIP, noise schedule, sampler, guider:** same files and settings as the official demo.
- **Gradient plumbing:** all trainable tensors receive gradients; gradient checkpointing gives bit-identical gradients; save, load and resume round-trip.
- **Context length for the loss:** 8 frames vs 21 distinct frames changes the denoising loss by only 2–7%.
- **Baseline precision:** plain SEVA's loss is the same in bf16 and fp16 to 1–2%. Precision breaks the gradient, not the forward loss.

## 8. What the past results mean, and what to do next

**Past results**
- No conclusion about *learning* from any run before today stands: not the date-pathway designs, the loss weightings, the learning rates ("1e-4 destroys SEVA"), FiLM vs LoRA, the no-date control, nor the ranking loss. They compared random walks.
- Conclusions about *plain SEVA and the data* stand: the camera chain, the census, the dating bugs, the ill-posed samples.
- The input-date shortcut and the unconditional-branch findings were derived from noise-trained models and need re-checking.

**Next steps, in order**
1. Repeat the two-sample overfit with dates as the target (section 6) until 2021 renders scaffolding and 2015 the intact cathedral. This is the gate for everything else.
2. Fix sampling so inputs see the targets: pick inputs near all targets, account for field of view, and stop forcing 30-year gaps where no overlapping old view exists. Replace the two ill-posed probes.
3. Rotate sideways images (with their cameras) and drop duplicate photos.
4. Re-run the single-scene baseline in fp32 with 21-frame, multi-seed renders, then the no-date control against date conditioning.
5. Then revisit learning rate (1e-5 was chosen under noise), gradient accumulation, and training at 21 frames.
